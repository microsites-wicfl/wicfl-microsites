## What is a home insurance nonrenewal?

A nonrenewal means your current insurance company has notified you that it does not intend to continue your policy beyond its current term.

A nonrenewal is different from a cancellation during the policy period. The reason for a nonrenewal can vary depending on the insurer, the property and the circumstances involved. Your notice should explain important information about the nonrenewal, including the effective date and, depending on the situation, the reason coverage will not continue.

## Why might a Florida home be nonrenewed?

Insurance companies periodically review their portfolios and underwriting guidelines. A nonrenewal may be related to factors such as:

### Roof age or condition

The age, condition and construction of a roof can affect whether an insurer continues to offer coverage.

### Claims history

Recent or multiple claims may influence how an insurer evaluates a property.

### Property characteristics

Older construction, electrical or plumbing systems, unique features or other property characteristics may affect eligibility.

### Coastal exposure

Homes in coastal areas may receive additional underwriting attention because of wind and hurricane exposure.

### Changes in underwriting

An insurer may change its underwriting guidelines or reduce its exposure in a particular area.

The specific reason for your nonrenewal should be reviewed carefully rather than assumed.

## What should you do after receiving a nonrenewal?

Don't wait until your current policy expires to start reviewing your options.

### Review the notice

Check the effective date, stated reason and any instructions included in your nonrenewal notice.

### Review your property

Gather information about your roof, construction, updates, inspections and previous claims.

### Start exploring options early

Different insurers have different eligibility requirements. Starting the process early gives you more time to understand what options may be available.

### Avoid a coverage gap

If you obtain replacement coverage, make sure you understand when the new policy becomes effective and how it coordinates with the expiration of your current policy.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have after receiving a nonrenewal?**
The timeframe depends on the circumstances and the notice provided by the insurer. Review your notice carefully and begin exploring alternatives as soon as possible.

**Can I get homeowners insurance after a nonrenewal?**
Possibly. Available options depend on the property's condition, location, claims history and current carrier underwriting requirements.

**Will an older roof make it harder to find coverage?**
It can. Roof age and condition are important factors for many Florida insurers, but requirements vary by carrier.

**Should I wait until my policy expires?**
No. It is generally better to begin reviewing your options before your current policy expires so there is time to address inspections, documentation and underwriting requirements.
