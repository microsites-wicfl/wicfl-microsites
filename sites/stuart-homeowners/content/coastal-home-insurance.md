  ## Why coastal homes have different insurance considerations

  Coastal properties can experience different risks than homes located farther inland. Insurance carriers may evaluate factors such as the property's location, construction, roof, wind exposure and proximity to water when determining available coverage.

  ### Wind and hurricane exposure

  Florida's coastal location makes wind and hurricane coverage an important part of reviewing a homeowners policy. Your policy may include specific wind coverage, deductibles or exclusions depending on the carrier and property.

  ### Flood and storm surge

  Coastal homeowners should understand the difference between wind damage and flooding. Standard homeowners insurance generally does not cover flood damage, which is why a separate [flood insurance](/flood-insurance/) policy may need to be considered.

  ### Roof and construction

  Roof age, building materials, hurricane-resistant features and other construction details can influence how an insurer evaluates a coastal home.

## What should coastal homeowners consider?

### Dwelling coverage

Your dwelling limit should reflect the estimated cost to rebuild the home, including its construction materials and features. The home's market value and its insurance replacement cost are not necessarily the same.

  ### Hurricane deductible

  Florida homeowners policies may include a separate hurricane deductible. Understanding when it applies and how much you would be responsible for is an important part of reviewing your policy.

### Wind mitigation

Hurricane-resistant features such as impact-resistant openings, roof construction and other mitigation measures may affect eligibility or pricing with certain carriers. A wind mitigation inspection can document qualifying features for the insurer.

  ### Flood insurance

  Even if flood insurance is not required by your lender, coastal homeowners may want to evaluate whether additional flood coverage makes sense for their property. Flood coverage is separate from standard homeowners insurance in many situations.

  ## Frequently asked questions

  **Is coastal home insurance different from regular homeowners insurance?**
  The basic types of coverage may be similar, but coastal properties can have additional underwriting considerations related to wind, hurricanes, flood exposure and location.

  **Does coastal homeowners insurance cover flooding?**
  Standard homeowners insurance generally does not cover flood damage. Separate flood insurance may be available through the NFIP or private insurers, depending on the property and eligibility.

  **Can a newer roof help with coastal insurance?**
  A newer or properly documented roof can affect how some insurers evaluate a property. Other features, including construction and wind mitigation, may also be considered.

  **Does every coastal home need flood insurance?**
Not necessarily. Requirements depend on factors such as the property's flood zone, lender requirements and individual circumstances. However, coastal homeowners should understand their flood exposure before deciding whether coverage is appropriate.
