## What can make a home difficult to insure?

Insurance carriers evaluate properties differently, and certain characteristics may affect eligibility, pricing or available coverage.

### Older roofs

Roof age and condition can be important factors when insurers evaluate Florida homes. An older roof may limit the number of available carriers or require additional documentation or inspection.

### Previous claims

A property's claims history can affect how insurers evaluate an application. Multiple or recent claims may make finding coverage more challenging.

### Unique or older construction

Historic homes, unusual architectural designs, older electrical or plumbing systems and certain construction materials may require additional underwriting review.

### Coastal or waterfront location

Homes near the coast, rivers or waterways may face additional considerations involving wind, hurricanes and flood exposure. Explore our pages for [coastal home insurance](/coastal-home-insurance/) and [waterfront home insurance](/waterfront-home-insurance/).

## Common situations we help homeowners review

A home doesn't have to be severely damaged or unusually constructed to become difficult to insure. You may need a more detailed insurance review if your current insurer is not renewing your policy, your roof is older or has recently been repaired, your home has had multiple insurance claims, the property has unusual construction or features, you recently purchased an older home, your property is located in a coastal or high-risk area, or you have had difficulty finding a carrier willing to insure the property.

Every situation is different, and available options depend on the property and the insurance market at the time of application.

## What information may be needed?

When a property requires additional underwriting, having accurate information can help the process move more efficiently.

### Property details

Information about the home's age, square footage, construction, roof and major systems may be requested.

### Inspections and documentation

Depending on the carrier, you may need a roof inspection, wind mitigation inspection, four-point inspection or other documentation.

### Claims history

Previous claims and the property's insurance history may be reviewed when determining available options.

Providing accurate information from the beginning can help avoid surprises later in the process.

## Having trouble after a nonrenewal?

A nonrenewal notice does not necessarily mean you have no insurance options. If your current carrier has notified you that your policy will not be renewed, the first step is understanding why the policy is being dropped and what options may be available.

## Frequently asked questions

**What makes a home difficult to insure?**
Older roofs, previous claims, unusual construction, coastal exposure, property condition and other underwriting factors can make some homes more challenging to insure.

**Can I insure a home with an older roof?**
Possibly. Eligibility depends on the roof's age, condition, construction and the requirements of the insurance carrier.

**Can I get insurance after a nonrenewal?**
In many cases, homeowners may have other options. The available choices depend on why the policy was nonrenewed and the property's characteristics.

**Will an inspection be required?**
Some carriers may require inspections or documentation before offering coverage. Requirements vary by carrier and property.
